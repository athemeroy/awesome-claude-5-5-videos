# Which visual effects fit Claude Opus 5.5?

**Research cutoff: September 27, 2026.** This is a production decision guide, not a model leaderboard. Opus can break down a brief, write and revise production code, and operate tools. The pixels come from a browser, Canvas/SVG, Three.js, Blender, Manim, Remotion, or external image and video models. Anthropic's [model overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/opus-5-5/overview) lists text and image inputs and text output. Its [launch post](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-opus-5-5) discusses program graphics, which does not establish that the model generated the pixels of a particular video by itself.

“Good fit” below means that a task can be specified, built with appropriate tools, and checked against observable criteria. These are engineering judgments informed by [source-linked cases](../data/cases.csv), **not measured success rates, quality scores, or a controlled comparison with other models**. Creator accounts, public source projects, and sampled X videos provide different kinds of evidence. Our [nine-frame method](methodology.zh-CN.md) cannot verify an entire motion sequence, audio quality, hidden calls, or physical accuracy.

## Choose a production path by task

| Task family | Useful role for Opus | Renderer, assets, and inputs | Failure test | When to bring in a specialist tool |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Product UI, brand motion, type and shape transitions** | Turn product information into shots, states, and timing; code SVG/Canvas/Remotion animation and revise pacing. The public [Hooklab brief](https://x.com/twoclipping/status/2102554209166000267) specifies supplied footage and UI, beat-level timing, and frame checks. | Real product UI, brand typography and colors, licensed fonts and footage, editable source. | Check text bounds, brand fidelity, adjacent transition frames, arbitrary-time seeking, and the loop seam. | If typography, brand detail, or complex compositing will not hold up in code, use a motion designer and established compositing tools for final polish. |
| **Process, educational, and data explainers** | Turn supplied facts into steps, charts, formulas, and a narration timeline. A [Manim calculus lesson](https://x.com/LinearUncle/status/2103128559174971663) and a [long Transformer explainer](https://x.com/dotey/status/2103683057689522564) illustrate the workflow. | Trusted source material, values and units, Manim/SVG/Remotion, and optional TTS and captioning. | Check every factual claim, formula, unit, pronunciation, and the full audio track; a finished image is not factual validation. | Obtain domain review for consequential claims; use reproducible calculations for numerical results. |
| **Abstract forms, loops, music response, procedural drawing** | Define deterministic paths, particles, brush strokes, keyframes, and transition rules. A [continuous-morph brief](https://x.com/leo_xiaolei/status/2102724347446305104) and an [open character-rig project](https://github.com/buildwithhanif/claude-animation-skill) show why rules and prepared systems matter. | Canvas/p5.js/WebGL, design references, beat or audio data, fixed random seeds. | Inspect adjacent-frame strips, loop closure, beat alignment, and repeat rendering of the same time point in different seek orders. | Complex acting, hands, or demanding art direction calls for a rig, human animation, or dedicated compositing. Count prepared songs, textures, and templates as inputs. |
| **Interactive 3D, product tours, spatial visualization** | Build cameras, lighting, controls, animation states, and capture/export. The [Clearwater WebGL2 source](https://github.com/Aureliengmz/clearwater) shows that realistic-looking water can be real-time shading; a [focus lab](https://x.com/RyanSael/status/2102591147927654847) demonstrates adjustable interaction. | Three.js/WebGL/Blender, explicit models and textures, fonts, camera requirements, and a device performance target. | Operate the scene, vary the camera, measure frame rate, check intersections and asset provenance; a still image is insufficient. | Supply scans, models, and rigs and use a professional 3D pipeline for photoreal modeling, character binding, or precise contacts. |
| **Stylized surfaces, paper craft, illustration, character shorts** | Build reusable style, texture, brush, layering, and shot rules; revise sequences against references. Both the [ant animation project](https://github.com/buildwithhanif/claude-animation-skill) and [paper-lightbox project](https://github.com/klsoen/opus-js-animations) disclose prepared systems or source material. | Reference art, style sheet, character sheet and rig, textures, audio assets. | Inspect pose, occlusion, expression, proportions, and texture consistency across neighboring frames; record human revisions. | Natural acting, detailed fabric/hair, or precise expressions need specialist rigs, simulation, hand animation, or suitable external image tools. |
| **Physical phenomena, natural motion, realistic destruction** | Plan shots, draw parameterized explanations, invoke solvers, and write export and checking scripts. A [particle-collider scene](https://x.com/superalesha/status/2102779758408774104) claims real magnetic-field trajectories, but its preview alone cannot verify the field data. A [building-collapse workflow](https://x.com/higgsfield_ai/status/2102863017109291059) explicitly combines Opus, Higgsfield, and Blender. | Equations or field data, units, boundary conditions, meshes and material parameters; a calibrated fluid, rigid-body, or cloth solver when needed. | Check conservation or reference trajectories, interpenetration, occlusion, full-timeline behavior, and repeatability using baked simulation caches. | For scientific, engineering, or film-grade physics, use a calibrated solver and domain review. [Blender's rigid-body cache guide](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/physics/rigid_body/world.html) describes one reproducible route. |
| **Live-action transformation, supplied footage, external video-model shots** | Direct shots, select takes, edit, caption, redraw, and coordinate external tools. A [talking-head redraw](https://x.com/AxtonLiu/status/2102827887732932956) retains the original performance and sound; a [multimodel collage brief](https://x.com/koldo2k/status/2103129347791986942) assigns image, motion, and music to other models. | Licensed footage, photographs and music, person/product references, video or editing tools, asset provenance. | Watch and listen to the full result; check identity, lip sync, shot continuity, rights, and which tool produced each shot. | Prefer recorded performances, supplied footage, and professional postproduction when reliable actors, precise product shots, or cross-shot identity are required. |

**A reusable production system.** The [Lemo-Opuscar repository](https://github.com/lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar) lists 39 styles and accompanying short films in its README, and publishes a [director guide](https://github.com/lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar/blob/main/DIRECTOR.md), [technique guide](https://github.com/lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar/blob/main/TECHNIQUE.md), and per-style instructions. Its [agent workflow](https://github.com/lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar/blob/main/AGENTS.md) calls for one brief before production and storyboard approval only when the user requests it. The maintainer describes code-rendered frames. This illustrates how a short trigger can invoke a prepared style library and production rules. The production history, quality of each film, and the precise “entirely in code” boundary remain creator claims; we have not independently audited them.

Three effect names show why the acceptance target comes first. **“Liquid metal”** might mean reflective styling, or physically correct volume and surface tension. **“Magnetic field”** might mean an explanatory path, or trajectories checked against field data. **“Shattering”** might mean a graphic transition, or material fracture with rigid-body collisions. Choose a shader, mathematical renderer, or physics solver accordingly. An MP4 alone cannot establish the latter kind of accuracy.

## Three acceptance stages

1. **Brief and keyframes.** Specify purpose, what the viewer must understand, frame size, duration, frame rate, shot and state changes, fixed text and brand elements, asset provenance, and any physical or factual constraints. Approve a storyboard and keyframe sheet before the full render.
2. **Short motion sample.** Render the hardest 2–4 seconds. Inspect 12–24 neighboring frames, occlusion, contacts, speed, loop seams, and caption bounds. For code animation, fix seeds and rerender a frame after seeking in different orders; for simulation, bake/cache the timeline. Check scientific claims against data and units separately.
3. **Full sequence and reproducible handoff.** Watch and listen to the whole film. Check duration, frame rate, resolution, text, levels, and synchronization. Deliver the MP4, editable project, render command, list of assets and services actually used, and remaining unverified claims. Nine-frame tiles are a screening aid, not final acceptance.

Copy the [production brief](production-brief.md) to record a specific deliverable, asset provenance, tool roles, checks, and costs. The [seven-path prompt playbook](prompt-playbook.zh-CN.md) is in Chinese and groups examples by production route, rather than by the seven task families above. A short human prompt may simply activate an existing skill, codebase, or prepared asset library.

## Compare costs against the same deliverable

When comparing pipelines, use the **same brief, duration, resolution, and factual and visual acceptance criteria**. Report these separately:

| Item | Record | Common confusion |
|---|---|---|
| Model usage | Calls, input/output and cached usage, and actual bill; mark list-price calculations as estimates. | Token price, subscription allowance, and completed-task cost are different measures. |
| External tools and assets | Image/video/TTS credits or bills, compute and render time, paid assets and licenses. | “Made with Opus” does not imply these inputs cost nothing. |
| Human work and rework | Reference preparation, storyboard approval, prompt revisions, failed renders, fact checking, editing, and final review time. | One human prompt may trigger many model calls and internal passes. Creator-reported hours need attribution. |
| Outcome and elapsed time | Time to first draft and accepted version, passed checks, and whether source is reusable. | Comparing first-draft speed or the existence of an MP4 hides quality differences. |

Show cash expense and human/machine time separately. If combining them into a total, disclose assumed hourly labor rates and compute pricing. [@mablesjoseph reports many calls and human revisions](https://x.com/mablesjoseph/status/2103465246014746943) for a short animation; those figures are a creator account, not an independently verified bill or model benchmark.
