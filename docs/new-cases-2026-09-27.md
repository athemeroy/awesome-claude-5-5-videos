# New cases on September 27: inputs, tool roles, and visible evidence

[中文](new-cases-2026-09-27.zh-CN.md)

This is a **separate incremental selection**. All original posts below were published after **2026-09-26 13:05 UTC** (21:05 China Standard Time, the previous search cutoff), and none is in the frozen September 26 set of 168 reviewed cases. These examples do **not** change that snapshot's 168 cases, 1,401 byte-distinct MP4s, or 1,511 candidate posts. We deliberately selected seven different workflows; they are not a random sample and cannot estimate prevalence or success rates.

We read the creators' posts and public replies, retrieved the attached X preview MP4s, used `ffprobe` for duration, frame rate, and audio-stream presence, and inspected nine evenly spaced frames from each video. “Nine-frame observation” describes only those sampled preview images. It cannot establish full-motion or audio quality, master quality, model calls, costs, or the complete production history. One small still from each X preview (one per attachment for two-clip posts) helps identify the visible style. Click a still to open its creator's original post; rights to the frames remain with their creators. This page does not republish clips, contact sheets, or others' full prompts.

## Reusable code and multi-tool production

### A personal story made with an open animation starter — @servasyy

<a href="https://x.com/servasyy/status/2104039075175182487"><img src="../assets/new-case-thumbnails/2104039075175182487.webp" width="320" loading="lazy" alt="Single frame from X preview: A personal story made with an open animation starter — @servasyy"></a>

- [Original post](https://x.com/servasyy/status/2104039075175182487), **2026-09-27 02:43:01 UTC**; the creator links [ClaudeAnimationBase](https://github.com/JohnHeibel/ClaudeAnimationBase) in a [reply](https://x.com/servasyy/status/2104039275298001049). The X preview is **80.043 s**, 24 fps, with audio.
- **Creator account:** The creator supplied autobiographical material and used the starter to animate a yellow square character with a green shoot. They report per-shot contact-sheet checks, 1,920 rendered frames, 22 minutes of Mac rendering, and no image or video model. At 24 fps, 1,920 frames equal 80 seconds, consistent with the preview duration.
- **Nine-frame observation:** The same square character appears across indoor, outdoor, fairground, and dinner-table scenes.
- **Boundary and use:** The public repository is a **general starter**, not the complete source of this particular film. It does not prove the creator's session, absence of outside models, checks, or render time. The case shows how an open character and rendering workflow can be reused for a new story.

## Data-driven video

### Token explainer with numbers bound to data slots — @arambarnett

<a href="https://x.com/arambarnett/status/2104011150471917838"><img src="../assets/new-case-thumbnails/2104011150471917838.webp" width="320" loading="lazy" alt="Single frame from X preview: Token explainer with numbers bound to data slots — @arambarnett"></a>

- [Original post](https://x.com/arambarnett/status/2104011150471917838), **2026-09-27 00:52:03 UTC**; see the [creator's workflow reply](https://x.com/arambarnett/status/2104011475379515628). The X preview is **20.053 s**, 30 fps, with audio.
- **Creator account:** Opus 5.5 reportedly received a token address, fetched live chain data, designed the chart and voiceover, and used a headless browser to render an MP4 frame by frame. The product code purportedly forbids the model from typing numbers: all on-screen and spoken figures must enter through live-data slots, with a failed check returning the draft for repair. Four-minute production and about $0.33 in model cost are creator reports.
- **Nine-frame observation:** The vertical sample shows cards for price, volume, holder count, and wallet concentration, with a source and timestamp in the footer.
- **Boundary and use:** The data snapshot, validator, full project, and cost record are not public. Sampled frames establish that numbers and source labels appear, not that the figures are correct, current, or actually slot-bound. The transferable lesson is the **design pattern of separating data variables from copy and rejecting hard-coded figures**, not an audited statistical or investment claim.
